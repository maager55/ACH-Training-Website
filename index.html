Level 3 Welcome

Welcome to Level 3 of the ACH training program. By now you have worked through the foundations of veterinary nursing and demonstrated the dependability, aptitude, and work ethic required for this next level.

You already hold most of the skills this role asks for: patient handling, medication knowledge, and teamwork. What changes at Level 3 is moving into the surgery suite, where the stakes are higher and the patient depends entirely on the team around them. By the end of Level 3, you will feel confident managing general anesthesia and understanding procedure-specific requirements.

Anesthetic Procedure Check-In & Discharge

Check-In Protocols

  • Confirm Fasting: Withhold food after 10 PM the night prior.
  • Mark Surgical Site: Mark site before patient moves to back (e.g., specific leg for orthopedics, specific growth for mass removal).
  • Obtain TPR: Record temperature, pulse, respiration. Confirm high/low aural temp with a rectal reading.
  • Obtain Weight: Weigh immediately; all drug calculations depend on this.
  • Label Kennel: Patient name, client name, procedure.
  • Notify doctor if patient was not fasted or has an abnormal rectal temp.

Surgical Instrument Quick Reference

  • Towel Clamps: Penetrating (first layer/drapes to skin only); Non-Penetrating (second drape).
  • Scissors: Utility (drapes/suture, never cut skin); Bandage; Suture (hooked blade); Metzenbaum (delicate dissection, bladder/stomach); Mayo (dense/heavy tissue).
  • Thumb Forceps: Brown-Adson (suturing/general tissue); Rat Tooth (fascia/skin); DeBakey (delicate viscera).
  • Tissue Forceps: Allis (crushing, tissue to be excised); Doyen (non-crushing, bowel clamping).
  • Hemostats: Mosquito (small vessels/feline/pediatric); Kelly (serrations 1/2 way); Crile (serrations full length); Rochester-Carmalt (large dog spays, longitudinal serrations).
  • Retractors & Suction: Gelpi (self-retaining orthopedic/neuro); Yankauer Tip (focal suction for soft tissue/masses).

Intubation Checklist

Preparation

  • Induction drugs (Propofol, Ket/Mid, Prop/Mid), Dry gauze, Sterile lubricant.
  • Selected ET tubes, Inelastic gauze, Laryngoscope, Oxygen mask.
  • Cats only: Sterile cotton applicators & Lidocaine 2% (20 mg/mL) applied to arytenoids.

Procedure

  1. Measure ET tube length from canine teeth to cranial margin of thoracic inlet.
  2. Ensure IV catheter patency & administer induction agent to effect.
  3. Use laryngoscope to visualize arytenoid cartilages, vocal folds, soft palate, and epiglottis.
  4. Place ET tube to pre-measured depth, position patient, attach circuit.
  5. Perform pressure check (leak appreciated at 20 cm H2O), start Isoflurane.

Anesthetic Drug Calculations & Formulas

  • Drug Volume (mL): [Weight (kg) × Dose (mg/kg)] ÷ Concentration (mg/mL)
  • mcg to mg: Divide by 1,000 (e.g., 8 mcg = 0.008 mg)
  • % Solution to mg/mL: Multiply % by 10 (e.g., 2% Lidocaine = 20 mg/mL)
  • Fluid Rates: Dogs = 5 mL/kg/hr | Cats = 3 mL/kg/hr
  • Reservoir Bag Size: Weight (kg) × 60 mL/kg ÷ 1,000 = Bag Volume (L) (Round up to next size: 0.5, 1, 2, 3, or 5 L).
  • MAC (Isoflurane): Dogs 1.28% | Cats 1.71% (Lowered by sedatives, opioids, and propofol).

Hypotension Treatment Algorithm (MAP < 60 mmHg)

  1. Check Anesthetic Depth: Inhalants cause vasodilation. If deep, turn Isoflurane down.
  2. Check Heart Rate: If low (bradycardia), notify doctor for Atropine (0.02 mg/kg IV).
  3. Fluid Bolus: Administer 10 mL/kg crystalloid bolus over 10 minutes if directed.
  4. Check Mechanical Causes: Ensure surgeon isn't leaning on patient or compressing caudal vena cava.
  5. Escalate: Persistent hypotension requires doctor direction for inotropes/vasopressors (Dobutamine 2–10 mcg/kg/min).

Arrhythmia Identification Reference

  • VPCs: Wide & bizarre QRS, no P wave, T wave points opposite direction.
  • Ventricular Tachycardia: Run of 3+ VPCs, HR > 160–180 bpm, weak/absent pulses. Urgent.
  • Atrial Fibrillation: Irregularly irregular rhythm, wavy baseline, absent P waves.
  • 1st-Degree AV Block: Long, constant P-R interval (>0.13s dog, >0.09s cat). Every P has a QRS.
  • 2nd-Degree AV Block: Intermittent dropped beats (P waves without QRS).
  • 3rd-Degree (Complete) AV Block: P waves and QRS operate completely independently. Slow escape rate (20–60 bpm).
  • Ventricular Fibrillation: Chaotic baseline, no organized complexes, no pulse. Emergency CPR & Defibrillation required.

Anesthetic Hypothermia & Active Warming

Anesthesia depresses thermoregulation. Cold patients metabolize drugs slower, require less inhalant (risk of accidental overdose), and experience impaired clotting/immune function.

Accepted Active Warming Methods

  • Approved Devices: Circulating Water Blankets & Bair Hugger forced-air warmers.
  • Warm until: Patient reaches 98.6°F, then remove active heat sources.
CRITICAL WARNING: Never Use Improvised Heat Sources!
Hot water bottles, microwaved rice/bean bags, gloves with hot water, human heating pads, and heat lamps cause severe, full-thickness thermal burns on anesthetized patients.

Brachycephalic Airway (BOAS) Considerations

Common in Bulldogs, Pugs, Boston Terriers, and Shih Tzus. High risk for upper airway obstruction during recovery.

  • Anatomic Concerns: Stenotic nares, elongated soft palate, everted laryngeal saccules, hypoplastic trachea, laryngeal collapse.
  • Action Items: Have several smaller ET tube sizes ready. Inflate cuff immediately upon induction to protect against regurgitation/aspiration.
  • Recovery Rule: Highest risk period is post-extubation. Keep in sternal recumbency, delay extubation until active swallowing, and never leave unattended.

Anesthetic Recovery Protocol

  • Extubation: Keep cuff inflated until patient actively swallows. Position in sternal recumbency with chin supported.
  • Monitoring: TPR every 30 minutes until sternal and normothermic (≥98.6°F).
  • Vocalization & Regurgitation: Alert doctor immediately if patient vocalizes excessively or regurgitates.
  • Pain Scoring: Note that ACH will implement formal Glasgow Composite Pain Scale scoring in a future update.
